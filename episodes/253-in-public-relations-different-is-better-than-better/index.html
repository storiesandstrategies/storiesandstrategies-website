In Public Relations, Different is Better than Better

Listen or watch: Apple Podcasts Spotify YouTube

What if the smartest way to stand out in public relations is to stop trying to be better than everyone else?

Recorded live at PRSA ICON in Orlando, Florida, episode 253 of Stories and Strategies features host Doug Downs in conversation with Sally Hogshead, two-time New York Times bestselling author and founder of How to Fascinate. Together, they explore how distinctive ideas, authentic strengths, and a clear point of view help communicators break through an increasingly crowded marketplace.

Why Different Beats Better

Sally's central argument challenges the traditional pursuit of superiority. Rather than trying to outspend or outperform established competitors, brands can win attention by offering something meaningfully different to the right audience.

As Sally explains, “Bringing something new to the conversation that hasn't existed yet is the single greatest way for us to break through in a distracted crowded market.”

She illustrates this with Boo Boo Bubbles, a child-friendly alternative to ordinary hydrogen peroxide. Its playful packaging transforms an intimidating childhood experience into something reassuring. Parents aren't simply paying for what's inside the bottle; they're buying a different emotional experience.

That distinction matters especially for PR professionals pitching ambitious campaigns to risk-averse decision-makers. Sally suggests presenting ideas along a spectrum, from overly safe to overly outrageous, to reveal why familiar tactics can be costly. When competitors have larger budgets, copying their approach may only make a smaller brand invisible.

Doug also asks whether artificial intelligence can help brands fascinate audiences. Sally acknowledges its usefulness but warns that AI cannot replace an original, authoritative perspective. AI can support creative work; it should not determine what a brand believes.

Find Your Distinctive Advantage

Sally also questions whether authenticity is enough. Simply being yourself doesn't automatically make you interesting or valuable. Instead, she encourages people to cultivate a distinctive identity grounded in strengths others can recognize.

Her memorable observation: “Committees are the death of an original idea because talk about lowest common denominator.”

She shares another striking example from a Florida surf shack near a drawbridge. Instead of treating traffic delays as a business-killing inconvenience, the bar offered discounted beer whenever the bridge rose. A disadvantage became a reason to visit.

The same thinking applies to individuals. Doug asks whether introverts can be fascinating, and Sally answers without hesitation: “Absolutely. And even more so in a world that is noisy, crowded, overstimulated.”

Thoughtful listeners, analytical thinkers, and people who build sincere relationships can be just as compelling as outspoken personalities. For independent practitioners, those natural qualities should shape the promises their businesses make.

Sally's Fascinate Test is designed to reveal how others perceive people at their best. During the interview, she offers listeners complimentary access through the Fascinate Test link in the show notes.

The episode's message is clear: in public relations, standing out isn't about becoming louder or pretending to be someone else. It's about discovering what makes you different, communicating that difference with conviction, and creating an experience worth remembering.
