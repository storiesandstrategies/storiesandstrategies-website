AI is Making the Oldest Skill in Public Relations Worth More

Listen or watch: Apple Podcasts Spotify YouTube

What if the rise of artificial intelligence is actually making one of public relations’ oldest skills more valuable than ever?

In episode 254 of Stories and Strategies, host Doug Downs welcomes Jennifer Bisbee, APR, president of Bisbee & Company, to explore why media relations is experiencing an unexpected resurgence in the age of AI.

After more than four decades in public relations, Bisbee has witnessed the profession evolve through countless technological changes. But shrinking newsrooms, declining trust in traditional journalism, and an explosion of digital content once had her questioning whether media relations was becoming obsolete.

Then AI changed the equation.

Why Earned Media Is Becoming AI's Currency of Trust

“Journalism and third party credibility is currency. Media relations is how we make deposits.”

That observation from Bisbee captures a fundamental shift in how organizations build visibility and authority.

When someone asks an AI-powered search engine about a company, product, or industry expert, independent reporting can carry more weight than what an organization publishes about itself. Third-party credibility helps establish authority in a digital environment increasingly shaped by AI-generated answers.

And media no longer means newspapers and television alone. Podcasts, digital publications, and other independent voices contribute to the information ecosystem influencing public perception.

For communicators, this creates an opportunity. Earned media isn't simply about securing headlines anymore. It's about building a credible presence that can influence how brands appear in AI search results.

But there's a twist.

AI is also changing how journalists and podcast producers receive pitches. Downs describes managing approximately 50 pitches daily across his podcast clients, with AI increasingly helping filter submissions.

Suddenly, PR professionals aren't just pitching people. They're also trying to get past algorithms.

Bisbee believes the solution lies in returning to the fundamentals: understanding audiences, delivering relevant stories, and making authentic human connections.

AI Can Change the Tools, But Not the Fundamentals

“So we've really got to turn them into with our own voice.”

Bisbee's warning about AI-generated pitches reinforces why authentic communication matters as automation becomes more widespread.

AI offers extraordinary opportunities to research journalists, identify relevant outlets, monitor emerging issues, and track potential crises in real time. But technology cannot replace human judgment or strategic thinking.

The conversation also explores crisis communications, choosing between boutique and international PR agencies, and responding when journalists publish inaccurate information.

Bisbee emphasizes preparation, clear messaging, and carefully evaluating media opportunities before participating.

Her perspective challenges the assumption that technological progress makes traditional PR skills irrelevant.

Instead, AI may be increasing their importance.

The organizations that thrive won't necessarily be those producing the most content or automating the most pitches. They'll be the ones combining intelligent technology with trustworthy relationships, compelling stories, and independent credibility.

Because in a world where everyone can generate content, being believed may become the greatest competitive advantage of all.

And that makes media relations more valuable than ever.
