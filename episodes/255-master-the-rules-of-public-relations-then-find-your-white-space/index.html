Master the Rules of Public Relations, Then Find Your White Space

Listen or watch: Apple Podcasts Spotify YouTube

What does it take to stand out in public relations without starting over? In episode 255 of Stories and Strategies, host Doug Downs welcomes Gaby Natale, three-time Daytime Emmy winner, the first Latina to win three in a row, author of The Virtuous Circle, and president of AGANAR Media. Gaby closed PRSA ICON with a message to pioneer, embrace your uniqueness, and break barriers. Her argument is simple: learn the rules of the game, and then find the white space that nobody has filled yet.

That is easier said than done when you are the one applying for jobs. Hiring managers say they want unique thinking, then hire the person who fits the mold that already exists. Gaby's answer runs through the whole conversation: your uniqueness is not something to hide until you fit in. It is the advantage itself, and in an AI-driven world it is the one thing a machine cannot copy.

Be a Pioneer, Not an Emulator

Gaby draws a sharp line between two mindsets. The emulator sets future goals based on someone else's past results. There is nothing horrible about that, she says, except that emulators don't move the world forward. They tend to perpetuate the status quo, and too often the status quo is not enough. The pioneer starts the same way, looking around to see what everybody else is doing, and then opens up to the possibility of doing something nobody like them has done before. That means believing in a vision before there are results to validate it.

“Your uniqueness is your gift to the world. And as a gift, you have to share with the world.”

That is easy to say and hard to do when you are the one applying for jobs. Hiring managers say they want unique thinking, then hire the person who fits the mold that already exists. Gaby's answer is blunt: the emulator can now be done with agents. The emulator can be executed with technology. It is your point of view, your taste, your voice, and your uniqueness that is going to make the difference.

Her practical advice is to stop waiting for one opportunity to validate you. Build your own evidence instead. Share your ideas, become a thought leader, and create a network of proof outside any single employer, so that by the time someone Googles your name, they already have all this information and you are not just one more candidate.

Visibility Is Not Vanity

Gaby's framework for that work is what she calls the pioneer's growth trifecta: excellence, consistency, and visibility, all three at the same time. Excellence means going way above and beyond, because the bigger the barrier you want to break, the more scrutiny your work will face. Consistency means showing up for the dream in an intentional and regular way. And visibility is the one she wants public relations people to hear most, because it is the work they do for clients every day and almost never do for themselves.

“Visibility is not about being vain, visibility is not about being frivolous. Visibility is a critical tool that we have at our disposal to raise the awareness about the contributions that you, your team, your company, your industries are making.”

Underplaying your own contributions to look humble, she says, is not helping anyone, and it is hurting all of us. The fix is not to shout at everyone. It is to be clear about your message and your intention, stay consistent with it, and let your aligned tribe find you. There are enough people in the world for your exact audience. The problem is wanting to please the wrong crowd.

That is also how you find your voice. Gaby points to Picasso, who started as a figurative painter using the same techniques as everyone else in his time, then took a little detour, then a bigger one, until he broke through with cubism. First he became really good at the craft. It is very hard to find your own voice in theory without putting in the practical work. The repetitions and the iterations are what show you which accidents feel aligned and which ones are not really you.

Doug's three takeaways from the conversation say it plainly. Be a pioneer, not an emulator. Visibility is not vanity. And master the rules before you break them. Learn your craft through practice, then develop a voice that is uniquely yours.
