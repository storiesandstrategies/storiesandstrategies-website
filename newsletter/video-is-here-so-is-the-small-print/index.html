Video is here... So is the small print

The Simple - Your listeners can now choose: watch or listen

Two of the biggest podcast platforms are now letting your audience decide how they experience your show, and it changes things for podcasters who’ve been on the fence about adding video.

On Apple Podcasts, a recent update lets listeners switch freely between watching and listening without stopping the episode. They can start an episode on a walk and pick it up on screen when they get home. You upload once, and Apple handles the rest. The audio and video come from the same file.

On Spotify, listeners go into their settings and choose whether they want to see video or just hear audio across all their devices. It’s something they set once, not something they change episode by episode. And if you want to get your video onto Spotify, you can do it yourself for free through the Spotify for Creators website, no matter who hosts your podcast.

The bottom line for podcasters: you no longer have to choose between audio and video, and neither does your audience.

Image generated by AI

The Complicated - Warning worth understanding before you act

Some podcast hosting companies are now offering to distribute your video to Spotify on your behalf. It sounds convenient, and it is. But there’s a catch. Once you set up video distribution through your hosting company this way, it may lock you to that host in ways that aren’t always explained upfront. As Podnews editor reported last week, at least one major hosting company launched this feature without even telling its customers about the lock-in.

This matters because podcasters switch hosting companies more than you might think. tracked 3,500 podcasts that moved to a different host in the last 30 days alone. The reason podcasters have always been able to do that freely is RSS, the open technology that powers podcast distribution. Your RSS feed belongs to you and lets you pick up and move whenever you find a better deal or better features. Tying your video distribution to a hosting company could put that freedom at risk.

Meanwhile, a broader debate is playing out on Mastodon (the open social network where much of the podcast industry now hangs out since Twitter’s decline). , CEO of TrueFans, is pushing back on the whole idea of platforms and hosting companies controlling how your content is distributed. His answer is something he calls a SuperFeed, a single feed that carries your audio, video and paid content all together, without needing separate tools or platforms for each. Blogs, events, merchandise and more could all live in the same place, owned and controlled by you the creator.

Video is coming to podcasting whether we’re ready or not. Just make sure you know what you’re signing up for before you let someone else handle it for you.

The Shift from Content to Asset in Podcasting

Most people still treat podcasting as a content strategy.

But a recent piece by Loeb and Loeb LLP (published via JD Supra) frames it differently: as an IP-driven business, where ownership, distribution, and licensing decisions shape long-term value.

That shift matters.

Because once you see a podcast as an asset, not just a channel, the questions change:

  1. What do you actually own?

  2. Where does your leverage come from?

  3. And what happens if the platform changes the rules?

The industry is still optimizing for reach.
It could be that the smarter operators are starting to think about control.

Podcasting vs. Streaming: They’re Not the Same Audience

There’s a reason your podcast builds trust faster than almost any other medium. New research from Magellan AI and True Native Media confirms that podcast listening is intentional, lean-in behavior where your audience has actively chosen to spend their time and attention with you, and that’s fundamentally different from the passive, background experience of streaming audio.

That distinction matters enormously if you’re using your podcast to build a personal or organizational brand, because engaged attention is where real influence takes root. The study also found that nearly 87% of podcast listeners aren’t being reached by streaming audio at all, which means your show is accessing people in a way no other audio format can replicate.

Amazon’s New Podcast Strategy: “Monetize EVERYTHING”

Amazon has quietly overhauled its podcast operation, moving audio-only content under Audible while launching a new Creator Services division focused on on-camera talent TechCrunch like the Kelce brothers and Keke Palmer. Rather than chasing ad revenue, Amazon is weaving content and commerce together, think branded merchandise, documentaries, and shoppable product recommendations all living under one celebrity podcast umbrella.
