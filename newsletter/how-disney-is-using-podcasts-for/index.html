How Disney is Using Podcasts for its Brand

Disney Is Using Podcasts to Keep You From Leaving

Nobody needs a podcast to find out Star Wars exists.

Disney+ and Hulu are doubling their video podcast library, and at Theme Park Scope reports that Disney calls the Mandalorian and Grogu companion show its biggest podcast launch ever, based on first week global views. Nobody lands on that show as a stranger. They’ve watched the series and want more time inside that world. Disney isn’t using podcasts for discovery. It’s using them to expand the experience and keep you in the building after the credits roll.

Disney also says 90% of viewers who watched the Tell Me Lies companion podcast streamed every episode of Season 3. I’d treat that carefully. It’s Disney’s number, relayed by a fan site, and it almost certainly describes superfans. People who seek out a companion show were already the likeliest to binge, so it shows overlap, not cause.

If you make or commission branded podcasts, here’s the takeaway. Most pitches promise lead generation. Disney’s model points to trust acceleration: hours of a familiar voice in front of people who already know you. Measure completion and return listening, not just reach.

Discovery gets someone through the door once. Disney’s asking what happens in the hour after.

Podcasting’s Biggest Study Can’t Tell You Where YOUR Audience Is

Sounds Profitable released The Podcast Landscape 2026 last week, and it did its job. Reach is steady, with 39% consuming podcasts weekly. Netflix shows up third among the platforms people named. Video keeps growing. If you read one study about podcasting this year, read this one.

Then read it the way a brand should. I run a podcast agency, so you decide if I’m being salesy or helpful and check it against your own data.

A study about the average person can’t tell your show what to do. Someone has to sort what applies to you from what doesn’t. What applies is that the typical consumer regularly follows only two or three shows. Your podcast isn’t chasing a slice of a giant audience. It’s competing for one of a few slots. And most people stick with one platform for their favorites, so being where your audience already is beats being everywhere.

What doesn’t apply is Netflix. Only 5% call it their most used platform and it carries a grand total of about 70 podcasts. That’s not a distribution plan for you.

The Landscape study also says Apple Podcasts is losing ground. The share of consumers calling it their most used platform fell from 11% to 8%. Before you rethink your distribution, know that the report itself notes this year’s answer list added Netflix, Tubi, Apple TV and HBO Max, which takes a little share from every other option. Some of that dip is the survey, not the audience.

And for your show, what Apple did nationally matters far less than what it does in your show’s performance. Your own dashboard answers the question no national study can. I’ll bet you a coffee Apple is your #1 listening app. Probably just over 50%.

For our clients, on average, the feed audience splits roughly 55% Apple, 40% Spotify and 5% everything else. We track YouTube separately, because most of our clients are brands and put the show on an existing channel as a playlist. That’s one client mix, not a law. But it’s the kind of answer the Landscape was never built to give.

Read the Landscape to see which doors exist. Read your own numbers to see which ones your audience walks through. And (sales hat on but tipped to one side) talking with someone who knows the landscape specifically for branded podcasts is extremely helpful.
