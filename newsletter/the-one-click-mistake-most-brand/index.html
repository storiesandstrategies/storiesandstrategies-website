The One-Click Mistake Most Brand Podcasters Are Making

Why that "Frictionless" Embed Player is Working Against You

Apple Podcasts has updated its embedded player for websites, which allows you to embed episodes or your show. The updated player supports video and trailers. When people listen or watch your episodes there it’s handy that’s not actually where you want them to experience your podcast ultimately if analytics matter. Here’s why.

As brand podcasters, we’re all promoting episodes on social media, and the instinct is to make it as easy as possible for someone to hit play: an embed sitting right on the page, no extra click to another app. But that convenience costs you the exact data you need. Your host will tell you a file was streamed or downloaded. It won’t tell you how far someone listened, whether they came back, or how they found you. Apple Podcasts Connect and Spotify for Creators can, because they sit inside the app itself: consumption and drop-off patterns, follower conversion, first-time versus returning listeners, discovery data. That’s the layer that tells you whether an episode is actually working.

An embed player can’t feed that layer, on either platform, for the same mechanical reason. When someone presses play on an embed, the audio/video streams straight from your hosting provider. It never touches Apple’s or Spotify’s servers, because the listener never opened the app. Apple says so outright: those plays never appear in Connect. Spotify hasn’t published as direct a statement, but their own guidance about embed “accuracy,” and reports that registration depends on the listener already being logged into Spotify in that browser, points the same way. Most people clicking from social media won’t be logged in. Either way, these platforms only measure what happens inside their own app or logged-in web player, and an embed on your site is neither.

The fix isn’t to add clicks, it’s to change the destination. I think you need the embed players on your website but use script to actively drive people to the apps instead. On social media, don’t just drive them to your website but give them podcast or episode-specific links on each Apple, Spotify, and YouTube. Still one tap for the listener/viewer, but now that tap opens the episode inside the platform, so the play gets attributed and the deeper data comes with it.

And I hate to sound like a commercial but the service I like is Linkfire. With this single link I can drive people to a location where they can listen to my podcast AND I get the analytics through Apple and Spotify. Linkfire has a free tier.

Canadians are Choosing Canadian Podcasts. Canadian Brands Haven't Noticed Yet

Canadian podcasts just pulled ahead of American ones, for the first time in 7 years. Canadian made shows now capture 43% of average listening time among Canadian monthly podcast consumers, compared to 41% for U.S. content, according to Triton Digital’s newly released 2026 Canadian Podcast Report. Overall listening is up too: 46% of Canadian adults now listen monthly, up from 39% in 2024.

Most coverage will focus on that growth number. I think the more interesting question is what pulled Canadian content ahead specifically this year. Two things stand out. CBC and Radio Canada dominate, averaging 2.16 million weekly downloads, more than double the next closest network. And Francophone listening rebounded hard after a 2024 dip, climbing to 33% monthly consumption, with 70% of what Francophone Canadians listen to being Canadian made.

This matches what I’m seeing on the ground. Listening is clearly up and Canadian content is clearly resonating. But Canadian brands have been stubbornly slow to treat podcasts as an anchor to their content strategy the way a lot of American and even UK brands already do. The audience data has shifted faster than the boardroom conversation has.

The old assumption was that Canadian audiences default to American content unless given a reason not to. That assumption is out of date, and the data now backs a Canadian first strategy. The brands that move on this before their competitors catch up to the numbers are the ones who’ll own the space.

Four Mistakes Branded Podcasts Often Make

A recent post from podcast host/SEO specialist Ausha rounds up four mistakes that quietly kill branded shows before they get a fair shot:

  • Launching a single test episode instead of recording five or six and committing to the run before judging it

  • Publishing on and off instead of settling into a cadence you can actually sustain, even if that means alternating full episodes with short teasers

  • Grading the show’s success after one month, before organic growth has had time to build

  • Rushing titles and descriptions so algorithms and listeners alike struggle to find the show

All valid points although I would add my own flavoring to that fourth bullet. The bullet, as written, is worded well but taking the concept to the extreme you end up with keyword stuffed bios and episodes.

Metadata matters, I’m not telling you to ignore it. But we’ve watched plenty of podcasts achieve significant visibility in the apps (using Ausha or PodSEO to measure it) with metadata nobody bothered to optimize, and plenty of others do everything right on titles and go nowhere. Discovery and visibility are way more complicated than keywords.

If you’re launching a branded podcast, pay attention to all four bullets, just don’t hang your hat on keywords as a magic formula.

Your Podcast Needs a Newsletter that Actually Does Something

Most podcasters who start a newsletter treat it as an episode notification service. New episode drops, an email goes out, done. That’s not a newsletter strategy, that’s a mailing list. The podcasters getting real value from a newsletter are using it to do things an episode alone cannot: build anticipation before a launch, deepen a point a guest only touched on, turn a listener into someone who shows up to a live event or refers a friend.

If your newsletter is not doing at least one of those things right now, it’s underperforming, even if people are opening it.
