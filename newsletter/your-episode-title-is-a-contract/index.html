Your Episode Title Is a Contract. Are You Honoring It?

Are You Delivering on the Promise in Your Episode Title?

Your episode title is a contract with your listener. The moment someone sees it in their feed, they make a decision: this is worth my time. So when they hit play and you spend the first three minutes talking about your day, plugging your Patreon, or reading a guest bio they could have Googled, you aren’t bonding with them as a friend… you’re breaking that contract.

You have about 30 seconds before a new listener bails. Thirty seconds. And if you survive that first drop-off point, you get another chance to lose them around the three minute mark. Every second you spend on housekeeping, small talk, or throat-clearing is a second you’re burning through borrowed time.

Hard truth: you’re not Joe Rogan. You’re not Mel Robbins. Those hosts can meander for the first few minutes because they are the draw. Their audience showed up for them. Your audience showed up for the topic you promised in the title.

The same goes for guest interviews. I’ve worked with hosts who spend three or four minutes introducing their guest before the guest ever says a word. By the time the conversation starts, you’ve already lost a chunk of your audience, and your guest sounds like a Wikipedia entry instead of a human being.

Think like a listener. The first question they’re asking is “What’s going on?” or “Why is this happening?” THEN they’ll ask “Who is saying this?”

Look at your episode title, then look at your transcript. How long does it take you to actually get there? If the answer is more than 60 seconds, start cutting.

Netflix is in the podcast game, but YouTube and Spotify still own the room

New research by Edison found that 14% of weekly podcast consumers have already used Netflix to listen to podcasts. Impressive for a platform that only entered the space in January 2026, but it's worth keeping in perspective: YouTube and Spotify sit at 64% and 42% respectively. Honestly, Netflix's numbers might still be riding novelty more than habit.

For me personally, the routine looks more like this: it's 9:30pm, I've got 30 minutes before bed, and I’m not opening Netflix. I’m on on YouTube, half-watching a podcast episode while I’m shutting down. Until Netflix can crack that kind of casual, low-commitment viewing habit, YouTube isn't losing its crown anytime soon.
